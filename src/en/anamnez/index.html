Anamnesis is a doctor simulator with honest diagnostics. Every patient has a real illness or injury, sometimes more than one, and almost every sign stays hidden until you ask, examine, or order a test or an X-ray. The game is in development; the screenshots are from an early version, which is in Russian.

How to play

  • A shift at the clinic. Patients come with and without appointments. The nurse takes temperature, pulse, blood pressure and oxygen saturation and sees urgent patients in first. The map shows who is where: at reception, in the waiting room, at the X-ray, in your office.
  • Examination. A patient chart with tabs at the bottom, under your thumb. The history is a conversation: you ask, the patient tells. The physical exam is a tap on a body figure. Lab tests, X-rays, ECGs, ultrasound, CT, MRI and endoscopy go on a referral form — each with its own cost and time. While a test is running, you can send the patient to wait and see the next one; those who wait too long leave.
  • Hypotheses. The patient chart holds a log of results and a hypothesis board: your own differential, and for each hypothesis what speaks for it, what speaks against it, what is still unchecked and what would confirm it.
  • Decision. A diagnosis, a treatment sheet and where to treat: at home, on a ward or in the operating room — or, if needed, a referral to another hospital or an ambulance.
  • Review. What it really was, a score for accuracy, justification, thrift and safety, and how an experienced doctor would have reasoned. Patients sent home sometimes get worse and come back, and the end-of-day summary tells you what happened to earlier patients.
  • Campaign. A young doctor at a village clinic, and then at a district hospital with an emergency department, wards and an operating room, where a vascular unit with a CT room and an intensive care ward opens, and at a city hospital with a ventilator ICU, an infectious diseases unit, endoscopy and specialist doctors. The first shift is with a mentor; after that come letters and tasks without deadlines.
  • Your own hospital. Offices, a lab, X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI and endoscopy rooms, an emergency department, wards, isolation boxes and an operating room, hiring, the cash desk. Doctors you hire see patients on their own, specialists have their own appointments, and new patients from the disease groups you pick are left for you. Insurance pays for a justified diagnosis and for tests that were indicated.
  • Emergency department. The ambulance brings in the seriously ill, and urgency is checked against the NEWS2 score. You do a ward round every morning and discharge patients when they are better; a surgical team works in the operating room — and the longer an appendicitis or a perforated ulcer waits, the more dangerous it gets.
  • Four difficulty levels. Trainee: “Looks like” hints, what relates to the complaint comes first in questions, examination and tests, and the review is gentler. Student: hints, and tests never err. Resident: up to three “Looks like” conditions with confidence in words, and tests sometimes err. Doctor: no hints, and tests sometimes err, as in real life.

What’s inside

The game’s medical database holds over 120 illnesses, injuries and poisonings, and it grows with every version. Patients come to you with over 110 conditions and injuries; the rest are poisonings and chronic illnesses that run in the background of the main one.

Clinic

  • Conditions a general practitioner sees: from colds and flu to pneumonia, angina, heart attack, hypertensive crisis, pericarditis and acute heart failure.
  • Rhythm problems: atrial fibrillation, tachycardias, heart block.
  • Pulmonary embolism, hypoglycaemia and diabetic ketoacidosis, nosebleeds, hives and anaphylactic shock.

Emergency department and surgery

  • Surgical conditions: from biliary colic to a perforated ulcer, bowel obstruction, deep vein thrombosis and acute ischaemia of the leg.
  • Injuries: fractures, knee ligament tears, a torn calf muscle, hand and scalp wounds, burns, chest trauma, concussion.
  • Wounds and burns as in a real emergency department: stitch now or delay the stitches, check the finger tendons, measure a burn with the patient’s palms. Tetanus shots by vaccination records, and after a dog bite, a rabies vaccine with immunoglobulin.
  • Operations — from appendectomy to hip replacement.

Stroke unit

  • Ischaemic stroke: thrombolysis within 4.5 hours, and the window closes while the work-up goes on.
  • Brain haemorrhage, transient ischaemic attack, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism — each with its own scores, its own scans and its own deadline.
  • A drooping face is not always a stroke: it may be Bell’s palsy, and then no CT is needed.
  • Seizures and status epilepticus: an anticonvulsant within 5 minutes.

Infectious diseases ward

  • Gut infections, meningococcal disease, herpes simplex encephalitis.
  • After a tick bite — tick-borne encephalitis and Lyme disease.
  • Botulism after home-canned food, viral hepatitis A and B, haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome.
  • An infectious patient never goes to a shared ward: there are isolation rooms for that.

Images and tests

  • The images are drawn by the program and differ for every patient: chest, abdominal and bone X-rays, abdominal, vein and artery ultrasound, 12-lead ECGs, head CT and MRI, spine MRI.
  • Decision rules from clinical guidelines: whether an ankle, foot or knee X-ray is needed, whether a CT scan is needed after a blow to the head, whether a leg vein ultrasound is needed for leg pain.
  • Signs don’t always show up and tests are sometimes wrong — as in real life. A diagnosis is a probability, and the review shows how sure an experienced doctor would have been.

Encyclopedia, profile and modes

  • “What is this?” for every sign and test in plain words, and more in the in-game encyclopedia. From a condition’s article you can work through a case — see one such patient.
  • A doctor profile: specialties with levels and small skills, practice totals, an archive of visits with reviews, and achievements — no deadlines and no streaks.
  • A “Shift” at a hospital of your choice and a “Case of the day” — one patient a day, the same for everyone; any of the last 30 days can be played at any time.
  • Two themes: the light “Chart” — paper and blue ink — and the dark “Monitor”. Or follow the phone.
  • Progress moves to another phone as a file.

Questions

How much does it cost?

It’s free, with no ads and no purchases.

Do I need the internet?

No. The game doesn’t even have permission to access the network: it sends nothing anywhere.

Is this a medical app?

No, it’s a game. The medical information in it is simplified and serves the game; it is not a reference, not a self-diagnosis tool and not a substitute for a doctor. If something worries you, see a doctor; in an emergency, call your local emergency number (112 in Russia and across Europe).

What age is it for?

16+.

What’s next

Coming up: new campaign chapters. The game is being prepared for release in RuStore for Android, in Russian first. Questions: support@gornitsa.games.
